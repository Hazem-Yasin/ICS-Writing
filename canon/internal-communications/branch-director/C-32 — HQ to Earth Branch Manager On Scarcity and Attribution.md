This is a good result. We have been waiting for a shared object of this kind on Level 4.

**Why not the other two.** A rota is an authority dividing a thing among people, and the authority becomes the thing they resent. Removing the bench takes from them what they already hold. Document 2.18 §2.1 gives the endowment effect: once a thing is "theirs", losing it is felt at about twice its value. Their anger would go to you.

**What we want instead.** We want their anger to go to each other. This is not cruelty. It is the one mechanism that costs the Branch nothing. A loss caused by a neighbour is blamed on the neighbour. Ross (1977) called it the fundamental attribution error: other people's behaviour is explained by their character, our own by our circumstances. A loss caused by an authority is resisted. Sherif's 1954 camp study found that two groups of boys needed no further instruction to become hostile once the prizes were scarce. Document 2.18 §3.2 supplies the rest: once a few people say who is at fault, the rest adopt it as the consensus.

**The plan, in four stages.**

1. *Relief.* Install three seats, so that the corridor has four, counting the bench. Your Liaison count of eleven is the demand. Do not install more than three. Specify tilt-back lounge seating (Item OBS-3). The tilt is a comfort, and it will be used in ways a bench cannot be.
2. *Observation.* Fourteen days. PTKW logs who sits where, for how long, and what they do there: eating, sleeping, reading, talking, weeping. Use the Wheel Scan on the AIDEN plush in the corridor. The fortnight will be calm. They will thank you. Accept it.
3. *Attribution.* From the log, choose the rituals that are most visible and most personal. Make each one the cause of damage. The damage is ours and the cause is theirs. Do not name anyone. The staff will name them.
4. *Closure.* After four closures, a fifth notice, permanent, citing misuse.

**Handling.** Nothing in stages 3 and 4 goes on a Branch-wide channel. PTKW Level 5 only, on your written order and not ours. Your public message in stage 1 must be sincere. It will be the most effective sentence in the plan. They will remember who brought the seats.

We are not concerned. We are noting the timeline.
