Colleagues,

I have been asked by the Board's liaison to look at the completion rate of the Support Steps, and I want to put my answer in writing before it becomes a meeting.

The rate is low. Fewer than one plan in six reaches the end. A reviewer from outside the project would see this as a failure of the instrument and propose a better plan: shorter, clearer, fairer goals. I have spent three weeks on that proposal and I want to tell you why I am not going to send it.

If more plans were completed, more files would close at Step 3. A file that closes yields no further observations. The people who complete their plans are the people who are no longer interesting. They have been *helped*. They go back to the floor and they are well, and they are, for our purposes, finished.

What we need is the opposite. We need a plan that is almost completable. The goals must be fair. A goal that is unfair is a goal that an employee can point to, and an employee who can point to an unfair goal has a cause. A person with a cause is no longer grieving. A person who is grieving is worth more than a person with a cause.

I wrote it down in the last year's review, and no one disagreed with it: *fairness is a design material*. The goals are fair, the support is real, the conversation is kind. The plan does not fail the employee. The plan holds the employee at the point of maximum effort for as long as the Board needs.

I do not enjoy having written this. I want to record that I did not enjoy it, and that I was the person who wrote it.

If you disagree, tell me. I would much rather be told I am wrong.

— Dr. Akar
