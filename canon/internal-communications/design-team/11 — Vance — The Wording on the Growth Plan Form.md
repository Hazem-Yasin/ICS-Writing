Akar,

Two weeks of text testing on ICS-PG1 are in. I am reporting the wording and nothing else, because the wording is my job.

**The headline.** *"This plan is not a warning."* tested best. It scored higher than *"This plan is a conversation"*, *"This is an agreement"* and *"This is a gift"*. People who read it felt calmer by 14%. They also read the rest of the form more slowly, and the signing rate went up.

**What I changed.** In the first draft the goals were in the passive: *"The employee will return to their usual range."* The testers found that cold. I changed them to the second person, and added "Your own range", with the possessive. The effect is that a person who has not met a goal is not failing a target but failing *themselves*. I did not intend that. I noticed it in the numbers and I kept it.

**The signature line.** I thought "I have received this plan" would be read as "I accept". Testers said it felt "honest". I added "I understand that signing confirms I have received it, not that I agree with it." It lowered signing by 3%. I recommended keeping it. I was overruled. It has been kept at the Board's request, because it makes the form more defensible.

**The 200 characters.** The comment box is capped at 200 characters. Testing says 140 is typical, so 200 is generous. At 200, an employee has room to say what happened and no room to say why.

**One thing I am not comfortable with.** The tea credits. We give four a week. A tester said, without being asked, that the tea made it "feel like someone was on my side". It is not that the tea is nice. It is that it is the only part of the form that costs us something.

I have been reading about biophilic design again. I am no longer sure what it was for.

— T. Vance
