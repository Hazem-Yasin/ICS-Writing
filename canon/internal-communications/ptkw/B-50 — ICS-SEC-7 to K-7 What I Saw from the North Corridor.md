K-7,

I was not on the floor. I was told to hold the north corridor door at 01:40, and to say nothing. I held it. I was the only officer on Level 7 who was already there when the Manager sealed it. I heard everything from the other side of a door.

At 01:56 I heard one set of footsteps. At 02:00 I heard the south seal cycle. For the next seventeen minutes I heard something I have no other word for than *work*. A rhythm, with a gap where a person stops and starts again. A breath at the end of it. I stood at the door with my hand on the bar and I did not open it, because the order was not to.

At 02:17 the others came in at the far end. I did not know what they were for until I heard the units. I have a body that has learned what the ACS-9 sounds like at 18 Hz, and my body told me before my head did.

I am worried, K-7. We were told, in every briefing since Day 5, that he could not be stopped. Now there is a Level 7 floor with a mark on it where he was stopped, and every officer in the building knows the number of us it took. Thirty-three. I do not want to be the officer who has to explain to the next one why it was thirty-three.

I will tell you the thing I am ashamed of. I am relieved. I was frightened of him for thirty-three days, and I am not frightened this morning, and I do not think I should be allowed to feel it.

Last night I delayed a sweep of the east corridor by twelve minutes, as I do. It did not matter. Nobody was there.

— ICS-SEC-7
