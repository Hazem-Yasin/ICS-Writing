K-7,

I am glad it is over. I am putting that first because I will not write it after I have slept.

I was at the second ACS-9. I have run that unit on forty-one floors in training and on assets in the field, and I know what it does to a person standing in the field. It does it to the one holding it too, at a lower level. At 02:27 he turned his head and looked at each of us in order. He did not need to. He had been told where we were. When he reached me he looked at the unit and not at me. I felt what it does. He stood in it with his arms low and his weight forward, and it did not stop him moving.

I do not know what I was supposed to feel when he went down for the last time. I felt relief. It was in my knees. I have not felt anything like it since a long-ago school examination, and I put the unit down, and an officer next to me, whose name I will not give, was sick on the floor.

Then I felt ashamed, and I am more certain of the shame than of the relief.

He had not hurt anyone with the intention of hurting them. The officer with the arm told me so, and I believe him. He had put his hands on the officers where he could, and he had put them down. We are the ones who made it last forty-six minutes. We are the ones who did it in the end because there were thirty-three of us and one of him.

I am going to my desk now. I will not run anything.

— K-12
