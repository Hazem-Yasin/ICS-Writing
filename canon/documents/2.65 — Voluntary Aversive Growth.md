> **1 — The Question**
>
> Section 2.9 of the main study records that comfortable humans resist. This supplement takes the opposite case, which the main study does not index.
>
> Humans who are not under compulsion, and not in danger, look for load. They enter cold water on winter mornings. They run distances that end where they began. They go without food for a stated number of days. They pay to be taught how to be struck, and then they pay to be struck. Afterwards, without being asked, they describe it as the best part of their year.
>
> The campaign design of the Branch assumes that discomfort is a cost and that a human will pay to have it removed (Section 1, Tiers 1 to 6). That assumption does not describe these humans. The observation needs an explanation before it can be filed.
>
> ---
>
> **2 — Sources**
>
> The Division read seven. Each is summarised in the Division's words. None is quoted.
>
> 1. **Seneca**, *On Providence* (first century). A person who has never met adversity has not been tested, and is to be pitied for it. The argument is old, and it is made as a comfort to the unlucky.
> 2. **Nietzsche**, *Twilight of the Idols* (1888). The source of the sentence that Earth repeats most often on this subject: what does not destroy a person makes them stronger. Document 2.38 already cites him. The sentence is the whole of the argument in the form that spreads.
> 3. **Mishima**, *Sun and Steel* (1968). An essay by a writer who came late to weight training and to kendo. He holds that a thing known through the body is known differently from a thing known through words, and that the first gives the second its reality. The author completed the argument in 1970 by a method he had written about. The Division records that his conduct was consistent with his text.
> 4. **Tedeschi and Calhoun**, "The Posttraumatic Growth Inventory" (*Journal of Traumatic Stress*, 1996). People who have survived severe events report change in five areas: personal strength, new possibilities, closer relationships, appreciation of life, and spiritual change. The share who report it varies widely between studies. The authors note that growth and distress are found in the same person at the same time.
> 5. **Seery, Holman and Silver** (*Journal of Personality and Social Psychology*, 2010). A panel of about 2,400 adults. Those with a moderate history of adverse events reported less distress and higher life satisfaction than those with none and those with many. The curve is an arch. A life without adversity sits on the low side of it.
> 6. **Maier and Seligman**, "Learned Helplessness at Fifty" (*Psychological Review*, 2016). The revision of the original finding. Passivity under prolonged stress is the default response. The experience of control reverses it, by way of the prefrontal cortex. Stress that the subject can end does not produce the injury that the same stress produces when the subject cannot.
> 7. **Goggins**, *Can't Hurt Me* (2018). A memoir by a former Navy SEAL and ultramarathon runner. His method is repeated, deliberate discomfort, which he calls callusing the mind, with a rule of thumb that the point at which a person judges themselves finished is well short of their limit. He gives the figure as 40 per cent. The text is not scholarly. Its readers treat it as instruction, and the Division found that it is applied.
>
> ---
>
> **3 — Finding: Three Conditions**
>
> The sources disagree on mechanism and agree on conditions. Load produces growth when all three of the following are present. When any one is missing, the same load produces injury.
>
> 1. **It is chosen.** The person entered it. The same cold, the same distance and the same hunger, assigned by someone else, belong to a different category in every study the Division read.
> 2. **It can be stopped, and it ends.** The person can leave, and can see how far from the end they are. Maier and Seligman locate the mechanism here.
> 3. **The meaning is the person's own.** The person decides what the load is for. A meaning supplied by the party applying the load is read by the person as a charge against them, however well it is meant.
>
> The Division notes that humans have found all three and built them into institutions: initiation, apprenticeship, penance, the training hall. In each, the person walks in. In each, the person can say what it is for. It is the oldest teaching technology on the planet.
>
> ---
>
> **4 — Why It Does Not Scale**
>
> The effect depends on choice. A load that the Branch supplies is no longer chosen, and a load that the Branch describes is no longer the person's own meaning. The Division therefore cannot produce this effect. It can only receive it from a person who has produced it for themselves.
>
> **The Branch's instruments against the three conditions**
>
> - **Support Steps (Document 2.53).** Chosen: no. Can be stopped: no, and the end condition is set so that few reach it. Meaning: supplied.
> - **Tier Velocity Standard (Document 2.56).** Chosen: no. Can be stopped: no. Meaning: supplied.
> - **The Comforts (the lift machines and the Wellness Hub).** These apply no load. No outcome is recorded against them in either direction.
>
> The Division records this as a classification and takes no view on it.
>
> ---
>
> **5 — One Hypothesis**
>
> A person who has chosen a load and finished it may carry less fear of loss afterwards. If so, the Endowment Effect and Loss Aversion (Section 2.1) would weaken in that person. The Division has no data and has not looked for any. It is recorded because the sources describe, in their own terms, a population that has made a habit of walking toward the thing the campaign teaches it to avoid.
>
> ---
>
> **Recommendation**
>
> No operational application. Do not extend to campaign material or to the Comfort programme. Retain on the Level 5 shelf as Supplement H. Closed.
