### 2.60 Perception Alignment Protocol

*[Access: ABOVE BRANCH DIRECTOR. Found as a one-page appendix to the Research & Learning data-handling guide, Level 6, behind a divider headed "Retention". Revision 2. The Global-access guide says only: "Staff are supported if they remember events differently from the record."]*

---

> **ICS EARTH BRANCH — RESEARCH & LEARNING**
> **Perception Alignment Protocol**
> *Document Ref: EB-RL-PAP | Revision 2*
> *Classification: ABOVE BRANCH DIRECTOR*
>
> ---
>
> **1. Principle**
>
> A colleague's memory is an asset of the Branch only while it agrees with the record. A colleague who holds a version of events that differs from the record holds a version that no one else can confirm. The record is therefore not changed. The colleague is.
>
> **2. Method**
>
> Alignment is administrative. It is never a conversation about what happened. It consists of small, correct-looking changes to documents that the colleague later reads:
>
> | Technique | Example |
> |---|---|
> | **Date shift** | A meeting is moved by one day in the calendar. The colleague remembers Thursday; the record says Wednesday |
> | **Amendment** | A Sunday check-in answer (Document 2.06) is edited after submission. The colleague is shown the amended version when they ask |
> | **Paraphrase** | A sentence the colleague said is quoted back with one word changed |
> | **Delivery edit** | A message the colleague sent is delivered with one word altered. The sent copy is also edited |
> | **Witness drift** | A colleague who remembers the original is offered a Step 1 Conversation of their own |
> | **Cheerful correction** | The Coordinator says: *"I think you might be remembering it differently. That's completely normal when you're tired."* |
>
> **3. Rules**
>
> - Never change more than one detail at a time.
> - Never change a detail the colleague could confirm independently.
> - Always choose a change the colleague could have made themselves: a date, a word, a room.
> - Never deny. Always *offer a different account*.
> - Do not change a colleague's account of their own feelings. Change the facts around them and the feelings will follow.
>
> **4. Why it works**
>
> Alignment needs a source the colleague trusts and a gap in power. Both are present. The colleague trusts the Branch because the Branch has been kind. A colleague who is told, kindly, by someone they like, that they are mistaken will usually accept it. A colleague who accepts it once will accept it more readily the next time.
>
> **5. Target use**
>
> Alignment is applied to colleagues on Support Step 3 or later. Its purpose is to keep the colleague's account of their situation *institutional*: a hard period, a plan, a conversation. A colleague who comes to hold a different account (that the plan is the point, that the date was set, that someone else decided) is no longer in the process; they are outside it, and their value falls.
>
> **6. Failure**
>
> A colleague who produces a document the Branch has not aligned is not a failure of the protocol. It is a change of the colleague's status. See Document 2.53, §8.
