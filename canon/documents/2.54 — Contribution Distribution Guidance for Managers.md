> **1. Why we calibrate**
>
> Every manager at ICS wants to see their whole team do well. Most managers can. The difficulty is that ratings, left alone, drift upward: each manager is generous to the people in front of them, and over a year a Branch becomes a place where everyone is "Exceeding", which tells no one anything.
>
> The quarterly Calibration Round gives every manager the same measuring stick. It asks you to place each member of your team into one of three bands.
>
> ---
>
> **2. The three bands**
>
> | Band | Description | Target share of a team |
> |---|---|---|
> | **Exemplar** | A colleague whose contribution is a model for others | 20% |
> | **Steady** | A colleague who makes the Branch work | 70% |
> | **Support Needed** | A colleague the Branch would like to help more | 10% |
>
> *Targets are guides, not quotas. A team may differ from the guide, and your Calibration Facilitator will talk it through with you.*
>
> ---
>
> **3. How to run the conversation**
>
> The Calibration Round is held in the Level 2 meeting rooms with your Facilitator. Bring your notes. You will be asked, for each colleague, *"What is the one thing you would like to see more of?"* Your Facilitator will ask you to compare colleagues with one another where you have placed them near a band boundary.
>
> *You do not need to explain a placement to the colleague.* The bands are an internal tool. Colleagues see their own Care Plan summary, not their band.

> **4. The target is a requirement**
>
> The bottom band is filled. A team that reports no Support Needed colleague is returned to the manager with the note *"no differentiation recorded"*. A manager who returns the same result a second time has their evaluation lowered under *Candour* (Document 2.59).
>
> A team of fewer than ten is pooled with a neighbouring team for the purpose of the round, so that the 10% can be reached. A pooled team's manager is told which neighbour but not which colleagues.
>
> ---
>
> **5. Where the band goes**
>
> A **Support Needed** placement opens a PTKW contact log (Support Step 1, Document 2.53) within 14 days. The placement is the event. The manager is not told this. The manager is told that the Facilitator will "follow up with a conversation".
>
> A colleague placed in **Support Needed** in two consecutive rounds is moved to Step 3 without a further conversation with the manager.
>
> An **Exemplar** placement is reported to the Research & Learning unit (Behavioral Data Analysis) for a readiness review. This is the colleague's reward. See Document 2.25, Case File #201.
>
> ---
>
> **6. What the distribution is for**
>
> The distribution is a fixed fraction of each floor, every quarter, at the same size. A floor of 140 produces 14 new files on the same day each cycle. A smaller number would put the pipeline at risk; a larger number would be noticed. Both have been tried.
>
> The managers who run the round do not know what the bottom band is for. They know it is hard. They say so, and they are told that this is why they are trusted.

> **Note to the Board, Earth review**
>
> The best predictor of a manager's loyalty to the process is the pain of taking part in it. A manager who has placed a friend in the bottom band does not report the band; they defend it. We have found that defending a decision is more binding than making it.
>
> The round is not a measurement of the team. It is an instrument for the manager.
