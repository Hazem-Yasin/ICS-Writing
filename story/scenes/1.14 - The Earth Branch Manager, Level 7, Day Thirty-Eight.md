# 1.14 — The Earth Branch Manager, Level 7, Day Thirty-Eight

*Cycle 300 Q4, Day 38, 01:40 to about 02:15 Branch Time. [PROPOSAL] The Earth Branch Manager, first person past, written into his own file as he wrote at Labor Source #312 (scene 1.1). The scene sits in the hour set out in `canon/internal-communications/branch-director/C-25` and confirmed in C-35: Level 7 emptied and sealed, two recorders in the bay feeding his terminal, the south transit seal, the Directorate as the Teshvar Consortium, fourteen of them, the speakers tested on the second night. The author's brief: that EOD falls, understands he has lost, gets up with his face bruised and broken, carves a smile into it with his hand and ruins it further so that it reads as a smile, and stands in a fighting stance that the Directorate does not know and that he and the humans know well, and breathes in it, and that the breath has a hidden meaning of struggle. I am setting this down in my own file because I do not trust what I will remember.

I left the floor at 01:40, by the north face, as I wrote that I would, and sealed the stairwell behind me with my own card. The light on Level 7 was neutral-functional. The display in the transit commons showed the live schedule. The coffee point was stocked with its two options. Sixteen chairs stood in the departure area, where they have always stood. I have done what I could for a man who will not know that I did it.

At 01:52 I sent the message on the PTKW channel, in my name, as I had drafted it. At 01:53 the channel showed that it had been opened. He did not answer. He walked out of the Board elevator onto the floor at 01:56, without a badge, in the suit that is wrong at the shoulder and the wrist, and went to the south face by the corridor he came in by on the night of Day 3. He stood at the bay door with his hands open at his sides. I watched on the two recorders that I had left. They are the only two, and they feed my terminal and nowhere else.

At 02:00 the docking collar cycled. They came in through the south seal in Teshvar-pattern vessels, fourteen of them, and they did not announce themselves. He answered them by moving. I will not describe the first minutes. I do not have the words, and they were not the ones I had come to see.

He lost.

It was not sudden. For some minutes he was what the file says. Then there was a moment, a little before 02:09 by the timestamp on the recorder, when something that was not a weapon reached him and he went down on one knee, and then on both hands, and then on his side on the grey floor of the bay with his legs still moving as if he meant to rise. I have seen men fall. This was not that. He fell as a thing falls that has been told, by someone it cannot argue with, how it ends.

He was down for nine seconds. I counted. The Directorate did not close. I had asked them to hold the floor to the south and the centre, and they held it, and I understood only then that they were also waiting. I do not think they knew for what.

His face was broken. The cheek on the left had come away from whatever it is laid over, and the jaw sat wrong, and the brow had a seam that I had never seen on the concourse and that I had not known was there. I had known for eight cycles that it was made. I had not said so to anyone, and nobody had said it to me.

He got up. First the hands, then the knees, then the whole height of him, which took longer than a man's.

Then he raised his right hand to his face. He put two fingers at the corner of his mouth on the left, where the cheek was loose, and drew them up. They did not go where he sent them. He tried the right side. He did it again, and again, with more force than I would have thought he had left, and the face gave, and what the face became was not the smile I knew. The old one went to the delivery driver and to the woman crying at the north desk, and was the same for both. This one was crooked, torn at one side and too deep on the other. Nobody would have called it a good smile. It could not be mistaken for anything else.

He lowered his hand. He looked at the Directorate for the first time as though he had seen them.

He moved his feet. The left went forward and out, the right turned and set behind it, the weight went low and down and stayed. He drew his left arm across his body and held the right back at his hip with the fist closed. The Directorate has no such stance. I watched their formation change round it, one head turning and then another, and none of them knew what they were looking at. I did. Any child on Earth who has been taken to a hall on a Saturday would have known it.

He breathed.

I watched his chest rise. It rose for a count, and held, and there was a short release through the mouth that the bay microphone caught, hard and flat. Again. A count, a hold, the release. I had heard it once before. It was in the recording from Day 21, under the voice of a captain in the air at 03:31:36, who held it against the load once every three seconds and was trained to.

I turned on the bay speakers. The Directorate had not forbidden it and had not agreed to it. I said the line I had asked the Team for, at the level I had tested on the second night, and it carried across the floor:

*"The Branch holds. ICS thanks you."*

He raised his face to the ceiling, to the grille the voice came from. He held the stance. He breathed, and the release went out of him again, hard and flat, and I counted three.
